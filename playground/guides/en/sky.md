## If you're just starting
Broken Sky is an isometric action RPG that runs in your browser. You mend the broken bridges between sinking sky islands, travel through seven chapters to the ending, and after that you farm gear on harder difficulty tiers and on Drifting Isles. The game is in open beta, so rules and numbers can still change. When they do, this guide gets updated with them. Every number below is read straight from the game's code, not guessed from play.

There are five classes. The **Shieldkeeper** blocks and shoves, the **Wandering Blade** cuts fast and parries, the **Windcaller** throws wind and lightning, the **Trapper** keeps distance with a crossbow and traps, and the **Bellringer** rings a bell for shockwaves and echo clones. If this is your first run, the Shieldkeeper is the most forgiving. If you'd rather fight from range, try the Trapper or the Windcaller.

## The four stats and where to put points
Each level gives you 5 points to spend however you like. This is what one point in each stat does:

| Stat | Per point |
|---|---|
| STR (Strength) | +1% melee damage, and the requirement for heavy gear |
| AGI (Agility) | Attack speed, critical hits and evasion (+1% damage for the Trapper only) |
| VIT (Vitality) | +4 life and resistance to status effects |
| BRE (Breath) | +2 Wind and Wind recovery, +1% magic damage |

### Suggested split per level (first playthrough)
- **Shieldkeeper and Wandering Blade**: STR 2 · VIT 2 · AGI 1. Melee classes take hits while they fight, so always mix in Vitality.
- **Windcaller and Bellringer**: BRE 3 · VIT 2. Breath raises magic damage and also the Wind you spend on skills.
- **Trapper**: AGI 3 · VIT 2. The Trapper is the only class whose damage scales with Agility.

Whatever class you pick, don't starve Vitality on the way through the first seven chapters. When a chapter boss keeps flattening you, a few points of Vitality usually help more than a gear upgrade does.

## Enchanting odds at the forge
At the forge you enchant gear up to +10, using Whetstones for weapons and Glue for armor. **Weapons always succeed up to +6 and armor up to +4.** Above that, a failed attempt drops the item one step.

| Enchant step | Weapon success | Armor success |
|---|---|---|
| +5 | Safe | 60% |
| +6 | Safe | 45% |
| +7 | 50% | 30% |
| +8 | 35% | 20% |
| +9 | 20% | 12% |
| +10 | 10% | 8% |

The table points to a simple routine:

1. When you get a new piece you like, take it to the safe step right away (weapon +6, armor +4). It only costs materials, with no risk of failing.
2. Push past that on **Blessed** gear. A blessing absorbs one failure.
3. Armor at +7 and beyond drops under 30% and eats a lot of Glue. Unless it's a Legendary or a Set piece you plan to wear for a long time, it's usually not worth it.

> Finish all three daily requests on the Village Board and you get a bonus **Blessed Whetstone**. Saving those up and doing your high enchants in one sitting is the most efficient way to climb.

## Gear rarity, identifying and curses
Rarity goes Common → Refined → Famed → Legendary → Set. Famed gear rolls 3 to 4 random options, and Legendary items are unique pieces with their own story. Good drops are unidentified until you identify them, either with an Appraisal Note or by paying 15G per item to the storyteller in town. Sometimes an item comes out Blessed (base stats +1) or Cursed (base stats −1, and you can't take it off until you purify it with Clear Spring Water). If you suspect a curse, identify first and equip second.

Gear you keep wearing unlocks a hidden option after you defeat enough enemies in it, and the forge can reroll one random option for gold. In the long run, picking one weapon you like and growing it is the surest way to get strong. When your bag fills up with junk, the forge's bulk salvage and the 🔒 lock let you clear it out without losing the pieces you want to keep.

## The seven chapters and the difficulty tiers
The story runs Dandelion Isle → Rusty Windmill Isles → Anchored Harbor → Upside-down Forest → Thunder Nest → The Sinking Old Capital → First Wind Hill, and then the ending. Each chapter has a village, a broken bridge to mend and a chapter boss.

Seeing the ending unlocks the next difficulty tier, and beating the Chapter 7 boss on a tier unlocks the one after it. Enemies get much tougher, and in exchange experience, gold and the chance of good gear all go up.

| Difficulty | Enemy life | Enemy damage | XP | Gold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | ×1 | ×1 | ×1 | ×1 |
| Stormcloud | ×2.4 | ×1.7 | ×2 | ×1.8 |
| Tempest | ×5 | ×2.6 | ×3.5 | ×2.8 |
| Thunder | ×9 | ×3.6 | ×5 | ×3.8 |
| Whirlwind | ×15 | ×4.8 | ×7 | ×5 |
| Great Wind | ×24 | ×6.2 | ×9.5 | ×6.5 |

Enemy damage climbs far more slowly than enemy life. So when a new tier feels like a wall, the problem is usually that things won't die, not that you die. If your damage feels short, enchant your weapon before anything else. Sky Charts that drop after the ending open Drifting Isles, which are short repeatable hunting grounds on whatever tier you're playing.

## Daily routine and the tailwind
- **Village Board**: three requests a day (finish all three for a Blessed Whetstone)
- **Today's Island chest**, the **peddler** whose stock changes every day, and **Airship Errands**
- When you come back after a break, a **tailwind** has built up. While it lasts you earn double XP, and it stores up to half of what you need for the next level. Missing days never costs you anything.

Near the edge of an island you can shove enemies off into the clouds. The Shieldkeeper's Shield Bash and Sweeping Shove and the Windcaller's Gust are especially good at it. Enemies that fall leave almost nothing behind, though, so save it for emergencies.

## FAQ
### How do I send beta feedback?
Open the menu (≡) and pick "💬 Send beta feedback", or use the button that appears when you finish a chapter. Your chapter and level are sent along with it, which makes problems much easier to fix.

### Can I buy anything with real money?
No. There are no real-money purchases, no gacha and no trading. Your save lives in this browser on this device. 5raksil coins, which you earn for free by playing and checking in, only buy cosmetic auras and footstep trails that don't change your power.
